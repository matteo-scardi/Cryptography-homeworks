Welford’s Recurrences & Sampling Statistics of Hacker Scores

Online mean, sum of squares and variance in three lines, applied to m hackers and n systems

📚 Exercise 1: Welford’s Recurrence Formulas

Following the same approach as Knuth’s recurrence for the mean, this exercise derives Welford’s recurrences for the sum of squares SS and the variance, implements them with a core of about three lines, and shows why they are numerically superior to the naive one-pass formula.

Mn = Mn−1 + d/n  |  SSn = SSn−1 + d·(xn − Mn),   d = xn − Mn−1

Everything runs locally in your browser.

Step 1: Knuth’s recurrence for the mean

From n·Mn = (n−1)·Mn−1 + xn:

Mn = Mn−1 + (xn − Mn−1)/n

Define the innovation d = xn − Mn−1. Then:

Mn = Mn−1 + d/n
xn − Mn = d·(n−1)/n

Step 2: Welford’s recurrence for SS

Let SSn = Σ(xi − Mn)² = Σxi² − n·Mn². Subtracting consecutive terms:

SSn − SSn−1 = xn² − n·Mn² + (n−1)·Mn−1²

With M = Mn−1, xn = M + d and Mn = M + d/n:

xn² = M² + 2Md + d²
n·Mn² = nM² + 2Md + d²/n

All terms in M cancel, leaving d²(1 − 1/n) = d²(n−1)/n. Since (xn − Mn−1)(xn − Mn) = d·d(n−1)/n:

SSn = SSn−1 + (xn − Mn−1)(xn − Mn), SS0 = 0, M0 = 0

Step 3: Variance and code

Population variance σ²n = SSn/n

σ²n = σ²n−1 + [ (n−1)d²/n − σ²n−1 ] / n

Sample variance s²n = SSn/(n−1), n ≥ 2

s²n = ((n−2)/(n−1))·s²n−1 + d²/n

Check, n = 2: s²2 = d²/2 = (x2 − x1)²/2, the correct variance of two points.

Python (three lines of core logic)

n = mean = ss = 0
for x in data:
    n += 1
    d = x - mean
    mean += d / n          # Knuth
    ss += d * (x - mean)   # Welford (uses the NEW mean)
var_sample = ss / (n - 1)      # unbiased
var_pop    = ss / n

Each update adds d·(x − Mn) ≥ 0, so there is no subtraction of large nearly-equal numbers. One pass, O(1) memory. For n = 1 the sample variance is undefined, so guard it.

Welford vs naive formula

Data: xi = offset + ui, with ui in [0,1), so the true variance is about 1/12 ≈ 0.083 for any offset. The naive formula is (Σx² − (Σx)²/n)/(n−1). The reference is the corrected two-pass method.

A negative or wildly wrong naive variance is the classic symptom of catastrophic cancellation.

📚 Exercise 2: Sampling Statistics of Hacker Scores

This exercise extends the previous one. m hackers each attack n systems and receive an integer score in {0, …, 10} per system, drawn from a fixed distribution with mean μ and variance σ². For every hacker, Welford’s recurrences from Exercise 1 give the sampling mean and sampling variance in one pass. These are averaged over the m hackers, compared with μ and σ², and the experiment is repeated for growing n.

E[x̄] = μ  |  Var(x̄) = σ²/n  |  E[s²] = σ² (divisor n−1)  |  E[SS/n] = σ²(n−1)/n  |  Var(s²) = (μ4 − (n−3)/(n−1)·σ4)/n

Everything runs locally in your browser.

Experiment setup

Each hacker gets n i.i.d. scores (one per system), up to n = 3000. The same trajectories are read at n = 2, 5, 10, 30, 100, 300, 1000, 3000. For each hacker, Welford gives x̄ and s².

What the comparisons show

  • Sampling mean: the average of the x̄j matches μ for every n. The sample mean is an unbiased estimator.
  • Sampling variance: the average of s²j (divisor n−1) matches σ². Dividing by n gives σ²(n−1)/n, a systematic underestimate that matters for small n.
  • Residual differences are sampling noise of size σ/√(nm) and √(Var(s²)/m), and shrink as m grows.
  • Larger n: the spread of x̄ falls like σ/√n, so halving it needs four times as many systems. The spread of s² also falls like 1/√n, with a larger constant for heavy-tailed distributions (large μ4), such as the U-shaped one.

Why this matters in inferential statistics

  • Estimation: μ and σ² are unknown in practice. x̄ and s² are computed from one sample, and unbiasedness says they are right on average.
  • Consistency (LLN): the estimates converge to the true values as n grows.
  • Standard error: Var(x̄) = σ²/n measures precision and is estimated by s/√n.
  • Confidence intervals and tests: by the CLT, x̄ is approximately N(μ, σ²/n), giving x̄ ± z·s/√n. For small n the Student t distribution is more appropriate, as the coverage table shows.
  • Sample size planning: a standard error ≤ e needs n ≥ σ²/e².
  • Computation: Welford gives both estimators in one stable pass over streaming data.
