Sampling Mean & Variance of Hacker Scores

Homework 6 – m hackers, n systems, Welford’s recurrences and inferential statistics

📚 Project Description

Each of m hackers attacks n systems and receives an integer score in {0, …, 10} per system, drawn from a fixed distribution with mean μ and variance σ². For every hacker, Welford’s recurrences give the sampling mean and sampling variance in one pass. The page averages these over the m hackers, compares them with μ and σ², and repeats the experiment for growing n.

E[x̄] = μ  |  Var(x̄) = σ²/n  |  E[s²] = σ² (divisor n−1)  |  E[SS/n] = σ²(n−1)/n  |  Var(s²) = (μ4 − (n−3)/(n−1)·σ4)/n

Everything runs locally in your browser.

n = 2, 5, 10, 30, 100, 300, 1000, 3000

Probability mass function

Per-hacker computation (Welford)

// one hacker, scores x_1 ... x_n arrive one at a time
let i = 0, mean = 0, ss = 0;
for (const x of scores) {
  i++;
  const d = x - mean;
  mean += d / i;            // Knuth
  ss   += d * (x - mean);   // Welford
}
const sampleMean = mean;
const sampleVar  = ss / (i - 1);  // unbiased
const biasedVar  = ss / i;        // for comparison

Experiment

For j = 1..m: simulate n scores, obtain (x̄j, s²j). Then compute mean(x̄j) and mean(s²j) and compare them with μ and σ².

What the comparisons show

  • Sampling mean: the average of the x̄j matches μ for every n. The sample mean is an unbiased estimator.
  • Sampling variance: the average of s²j (divisor n−1) matches σ². Dividing by n instead gives σ²(n−1)/n, a systematic underestimate that matters for small n.
  • Residual differences are sampling noise of size σ/√(nm) and √(Var(s²)/m), and shrink with m.
  • Larger n: each hacker’s x̄ and s² get closer to μ and σ². The spread of x̄ falls like σ/√n, so halving it needs four times as many systems. The spread of s² falls like 1/√n too, with a larger constant when the distribution has heavy tails (large μ4).

Why this matters in inferential statistics

  • Estimation: μ and σ² are unknown in practice. x̄ and s² are computed from one sample, and unbiasedness says they are right on average.
  • Consistency (LLN): the estimates converge to the true values as n grows.
  • Standard error: Var(x̄) = σ²/n lets us measure precision, estimated by s/√n.
  • Confidence intervals and tests: by the CLT, x̄ is approximately N(μ, σ²/n), giving x̄ ± z·s/√n. For small n the Student t distribution is more appropriate, as the coverage table shows.
  • Sample size planning: to get standard error ≤ e one needs n ≥ σ²/e².
  • Computation: Welford gives both estimators in one stable pass over streaming data.
