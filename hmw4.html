Penetration Scores: LLN & CLT

Homework 3 – n systems, m attackers, sums of i.i.d. increments

📚 Project Description & Theory

Each of m attackers faces n systems and earns an i.i.d. score Xi on each one. Two models are compared: Bernoulli (X∈{0,1}, P(X=1)=p) and the symmetric zero-mean Rademacher (X∈{−1,+1}, probability 1/2 each). The page simulates m trajectories and studies Sn, Sn/n and Sn/√n.

Var(Sn) = nσ²  |  Var(Sn/n) = σ²/n → 0  |  Var(Sn/√n) = σ²  |  LLN: Sn/n → μ in probability  |  CLT: (Sn−nμ)/(σ√n) → N(0,1)

Bernoulli: μ=p, σ²=p(1−p). Rademacher: μ=0, σ²=1, so Sn/√n is already standardized. Everything runs locally in your browser.

Both models are always simulated (n up to 10,000 for every attacker). Changing p or m requires a new run.

Chebyshev always holds but is loose. The real tail decays exponentially in n (Hoeffding), and the CLT approximation tracks it much better for large n.

Interpretation

  • Model: attacker j earns Xj,i on system i. Sn(j) is its aggregate score and the m attackers are m independent copies of Sn.
  • System size n: the total score grows like nμ but fluctuates only like σ√n. Per system, the score concentrates on μ (LLN) with relative error ∼ 1/√n. With many systems, attackers become statistically indistinguishable.
  • Symmetric case: when μ = 0 there is no systematic gain. The total score is a pure random walk of size √n and Sn/√n is asymptotically N(0,1).
  • Number of attackers m: it does not change the law of Sn. It sharpens the estimate of the distribution and pushes the best outcome up like σ√(2n ln m). The best attacker's average advantage over the mean, divided by n, vanishes as 1/√n.
  • Limits: real attackers learn, vulnerabilities are shared between systems and attackers are not independent. All of this breaks the i.i.d. assumption.
